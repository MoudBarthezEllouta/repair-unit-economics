# Decision memo: lower labor cost does not establish a scale case

**Recommendation:** keep the synthetic pilot below scale and investigate housing
failures before proposing another controlled trial.

The pilot's €24.17 cost per started case looks attractive against €27.11 for the
standard route. Its yield is only 85.40%, however, and cost per sellable unit is
€28.30 versus €28.66. The apparent advantage is much smaller once unsuccessful
work is included. Its yield fails the example's 90% threshold.

Inspect the failure Pareto, then stratify by route and failure family. The generator
intentionally assigns lower success probability to pilot housing cases. That is a
known simulation mechanism, not a discovered causal effect in a real factory.

For an actual follow-up, first validate failure labels and repair-episode boundaries.
Predefine a quality non-inferiority margin and economic hurdle, calculate sample
size, and randomize eligible devices within model and failure strata. Include repeat
repair, warranty, resale grade and lead-time guardrails. Declare a stop rule before
launch. A 50-case threshold alone is not a power calculation.

Interview walkthrough: explain why the denominator changes the decision, why
failed work remains in cost, and what evidence is still missing before scaling.
